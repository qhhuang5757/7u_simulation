The particle-count chart is the reference for equilibrium timing. Following the Siyavula rate-time model, each disturbance produces an immediate rate change, followed by a smooth adjustment until the rates become equal again. Adding N2 or H2 immediately raises only the forward rate; as NH3 forms, backward rises and forward falls. Adding NH3 immediately raises only backward; as NH3 is consumed, forward rises and backward falls. Removing N2 or H2 immediately lowers forward; the reverse reaction then restores reactants, so forward rises and backward falls. Removing NH3 immediately lowers backward; the forward reaction reforms NH3, so backward rises and forward falls. Pressure and temperature changes immediately affect both rates, with the favoured direction changing more. The new common equilibrium rate depends on the new composition and conditions. Temperature dependence uses an illustrative Arrhenius factor because measured activation-energy data were not supplied. Qp/Kp remains visible as a chemistry diagnostic.
Model note
This is an educational, stochastic particle illustration rather than a quantitatively calibrated molecular-dynamics simulation. At equilibrium, molecules continue moving and colliding. In a real system, instantaneous particle counts can fluctuate. The Haber process is exothermic, so increasing temperature favours the reverse direction.
